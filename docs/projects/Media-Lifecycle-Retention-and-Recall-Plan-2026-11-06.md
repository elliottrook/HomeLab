# Media retention and recall candidate — 2026-11-06

> Planning and feasibility only. No media deletion, unmonitoring, profile change, schedule activation, placeholder or request action was performed.

## Latest direction and scope boundary

Jason selected a 1080p maximum and **45 decimal MB/minute soft planning reference for all Archive movies and TV**, one best acceptable English 5.1 track when genuinely available, genuine HDR where present, and all English subtitles. This replaces the earlier 5 GB per-file ceiling and the proposed 720p/stereo TV alternative in the *design*. Archive eligibility and its transcoding step move from four to **three calendar months** after the present movie file was acquired or the youngest present episode in a TV season was acquired. Current and Archive remain views; a new acquisition stays in Current at its acquired size during that three-month interval. The separate **first-ever title acquisition date** controls oldest-first deletion order. Quality-led output may be above 45 MB/min when the verified source demands it; do not upscale, upmix or invent HDR from legacy media.

Jason also requested a **plan** for automated deletion of the oldest never-watched Archive material when pool use approaches a 60–62% band, retaining title identities in Radarr/Sonarr but unmonitoring removed media. This is a material expansion of the existing Stream A scope, which excluded irreversible bulk removal. Only planning and read-only feasibility work are authorized here; production deletion needs a separate explicit risk decision after the pilot and recovery packet are concrete.

## Feasibility baseline

- Live `Media` pool is ONLINE at 41% raw allocation: 39.978 TB raw size, 16.766 TB allocated, 15.322 TB ZFS available to datasets. At today's raw-to-usable ratio, 60% raw use is about **4.77 TB additional usable growth**, and the 60–62% band is only about **0.53 TB usable**. At 2.0 TB/month unoptimized intake that band fills in roughly eight days; at a hypothetical 1.64 TB/month all-1080p Archive-equivalent rate, about ten days. A daily controller is appropriate, but a large file, delayed snapshot release or concurrent download can still move usage outside the band.
- Uniformly sizing the present Archive's 306,876 measured minutes at 45 MB/min yields about **13.809 TB** versus its present 5.263 TB, an 8.547 TB expansion. If this genuine-upgrade scenario were completed immediately with no deletions, estimated raw pool use would be about **74.3%**. Returning to 62% would require about **3.25 TB usable reclamation**; returning to 60%, about **3.78 TB**. This is a mathematical scenario, not a feasible bulk conversion claim: most present SD/720p/stereo files have no verified higher-quality source and must not be artificially upscaled/upmixed.
- Administrator-scoped Jellyfin audit across all **seven** users found 5,997 indexed Archive video files. A file was protected if any user had `Played`, `PlayCount`, `LastPlayedDate`, playback progress or a favorite. This protected 346 files, 0.730 TB of current apparent size. The **upper bound** of unplayed, non-favorite indexed media is 920 movies/1.609 TB and 4,731 TV episode files/2.918 TB; whole-unplayed TV seasons cover 2.773 TB and whole-unplayed series 2.085 TB. Seven unindexed physical files and any unknown watch state are protected by default. These are *upper bounds*, not a deletion list: watch history may predate the present Jellyfin identity, legacy first-acquisition dates are not yet proven, and ARR ownership is still being reconciled.
- The current Archive contains 22 oversized movies under the superseded 5 GB rule, 32 matched but unregistered movies, 33 alternate movie copies and 173 movies without a unique provider match. The broader original 929-file ARR ownership gap is not closed. No automatic deletion should run against an unreconciled file or shared-folder conflict.

## Proposed first part: lifecycle and capacity controller

1. **Persist facts before any removal.** Create a source-local durable lifecycle ledger keyed by provider ID plus ARR ID and file identity. Keep distinct **first-ever title acquisition date** for deletion ranking and **current-file acquisition date** for the three-month view/optimizer clock, plus source quality/streams, Jellyfin item/user watch-state digest, favorite/keep flags, English subtitle manifest, path, size, state transitions, deletion reason, and request/reacquisition cooldown. ARR history can seed recent acquisitions, but migration-era `dateAdded`, filesystem mtime and later ARR re-registration do not prove the original first acquisition of legacy files. Jason subsequently approved a labeled media-date fallback for eviction ordering when first acquisition is unknown: movie release date/year, or original TV season/series air date. It must not be stored as acquisition history or used for the three-month clock; unknown media dates remain held. Keep private user watch data source-local, with only aggregate evidence in Git.
2. **Acquire under the existing ARR quality policy.** ARR remains the acquisition authority. No mass search or profile rewrite occurs as a side effect of the retention controller. A new acquisition remains in Current at its acquired quality and size for three calendar months. A movie becomes Archive-eligible three calendar months after its **current file** was imported, including an upgrade or re-request. A TV **season** becomes eligible only when its youngest present episode file is three calendar months old; a new or upgraded episode resets that season's clock, not the whole show. Only at eligibility does the quality-led Archive optimizer run. An optimizer's in-place replacement must not reset its own eligibility clock.
3. **Optimize once, quality first.** For archive-eligible titles, attempt a 1080p HEVC/Main10 output with one best English 5.1 track when source has genuine 5.1, genuine HDR when source has it, and every English subtitle variant and sidecar. The 45 MB/min value is an anomaly/planning reference, not a bitrate command. Reject a staged output with poorer-than-accepted picture, damaged playback, missing subtitle/flags, lost HDR/5.1, incorrect ARR identity, or a larger file without justified quality benefit. Legacy SD/stereo stays playable and monitored for a genuine upgrade; conversion never manufactures it.
4. **Measure the whole pool.** Use TrueNAS `zpool` raw allocation as the 60–62% authority, with `zfs available`, snapshot-held bytes, ARR/Jellyfin health and pending-download bytes as corroboration. At **60%**, begin bounded deletion of prevalidated candidates, oldest eligible first, aiming back near **60%**; **62%** is the upper guardrail that triggers an urgent alert and intake review if the controller cannot keep up. Protect the 0.53 TB band from a single oversized action by predicting but never assuming freed bytes; re-read actual pool use between batches. The three-month unoptimized Current intake must be included in the capacity forecast and candidate queue. If space is held by ZFS snapshots or ARR recycle bins, do not keep deleting merely because capacity has not fallen. Report a failure to reach target rather than deleting watched or unknown material.
5. **Fail-closed candidate rules.** Limit to Archive media older than three months or verified legacy Archive members, with a verified ARR file record, exact unique Jellyfin path/provider identity, all seven users' watch state available, no watched/partly watched/favorite/keep state, no active playback or download, and no unresolved duplicate or subtitle sidecar. Rank by validated *first acquisition* date, falling back only when absent to provider-backed movie release or TV season/series air date, then stable provider ID. Record date source and precision; media age is not acquisition age. Movie is one unit; TV uses a whole season so episode navigation remains coherent. Unmonitor only the selected Sonarr season/episodes; keep the series record and other seasons' monitoring. If all seasons go, keep but unmonitor the show. Protect re-requested titles for a defined cooldown so the oldest-first date cannot delete them immediately again.
6. **Stage and execute with recovery.** Shadow-run the exact candidate order and predicted bytes first. For a bounded production pilot, capture ARR/Jellyfin/media checkpoints and a restore proof, notify/offer a grace period, then re-evaluate the candidate immediately before action. Unmonitor in ARR before deleting its **file record via ARR's file API**; verify physical file disappearance, ARR tombstone, no automatic reacquisition, Jellyfin rescan and household watch continuity. Retain protected rollback material for an agreed short period on a capacity-accounted recovery location, then release it. Do not claim reclaimed space while a same-pool snapshot or recycle bin still holds the bytes. Bound each run by files, bytes and time; stop on any identity, watch, storage or API surprise. A Doctor alert should cover backlog, eligible capacity, stuck snapshots and failure to hold the band.

## Second part: unavailable marker and request feasibility

Removing the real file makes the normal Jellyfin item disappear on a rescan. A persistent request card therefore needs a separate catalog entry or a custom AsterTV view. The retained ARR record and provider IDs are the source of truth for missing/requestable state; Seerr remains the household film/TV request authority. [Seerr's API](https://docs.seerr.dev/api/update-media-status/) exposes a media availability status update, including `deleted`, but its interaction with a still-present unmonitored ARR record must be proven in a lab before use.

| Route | Feasibility | Main issue |
|---|---|---|
| **AsterTV/Kodi catalog card over retained ARR/Seerr identity** | Recommended first pilot. Render a monochrome poster and explicit **Request again** action for a missing title. An authenticated backend rechecks pool headroom, idempotence, cooldown and profile; records the request in Seerr, flips only the needed ARR monitoring fields and runs one bounded movie/season/episode search. Then play a short pre-rendered status clip using Aster's canonical voice. The card can show Pending, Searching, Downloading, Ready or Failed. | Card is in AsterTV, not a native Jellyfin library. Requires v3 client coordination and visual/remote-control acceptance. |
| **Dedicated Jellyfin placeholder library** | Possible lab pilot: metadata/NFO/provider IDs plus a small generated monochrome poster and short video or `.strm` item, stored **outside ARR roots**. The clip can announce the request state. | A played clip is not an authenticated request action. Jellyfin/Kodi may index it as available media, pollute Resume/watch history or confuse Seerr availability. A playback event/plugin or AsterTV client action must handle explicit authenticated confirmation. Do not put a fake feature in ARR's movie/TV folders or rely on a `.strm` GET as a write trigger. |
| **Jellyfin server/client plugin with virtual items and request action** | Highest fidelity to a native Jellyfin marker if the client supports the extension. | Substantial version/client-specific development, no demonstrated support across the household Kodi and Jellyfin clients; requires an isolated proof. |

The AI clip can be **generated once** as a reusable six-to-ten-second status asset with the lab's canonical Aster voice, then played locally. A per-title online generation call adds latency, failure and privacy exposure without improving the request decision. Selection should show an explicit confirmation before any search, because starting playback can be triggered by previews, retries or accidental remote presses. A re-request must be idempotent, rate-limited and signed to a household identity; clients must never hold ARR API keys. A marker is removed only after the real file, ARR record, Jellyfin visibility and playback all verify.
